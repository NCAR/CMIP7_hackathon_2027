About

CMIP7 offers a new opportunity to confront Earth system models with observations. This hackathon will analyze CMIP7 simulations as they become available in early January 2027, examining how well this new generation of models represents historical trends and multidecadal variability, including both discrepancies and successes.

The aim is to separate externally forced change from internally generated variability, identify the physical processes involved, and produce focused publications ahead of the IPCC AR7 deadline (March 15, 2027).

Build on recent work

Starting from lessons learned in the observed-versus-modeled trend comparison of Simpson, Shaw et al. (2025).

Focused teams

Sub-groups tackle specific variables or regions, with most of the time spent hacking.

Learn the tools

Daily lectures and tutorials on CMIP data, Jupyter, GitHub, and good coding practice.

Inform IPCC AR7

Each team targets a publication placing CMIP7 in the context of earlier ensembles.

Apply

Participation is by application, and applications are due {{ e.application_deadline }}. The hackathon is open to all career stages.

Capacity at each hub is limited, and a limited amount of travel support is available thanks to Spark Climate Solutions and the CMIP IPO. Priority for travel support will be given to early-career researchers (within 10 years of PhD).

If we receive more applications than places, or more requests for travel support than funding available, we will select participants based on career stage and the relevance of their interests and proposed research to the hackathon goals.
